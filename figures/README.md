# Objective 2 Visualizations Guide

This guide explains what each table and graph shows, and demonstrates why **S2 (Smart Levy) is better than S1 (Mandate) and S0 (BAU)**.

---

## Summary Tables

### 1. Cumulative Scenario Summary (2026–2050)

**File**: `table_cumulative_summary.png`

**What it shows**: Total costs, emissions, and efficiency metrics across all three scenarios for the entire modeling period (2026–2050).

**Key columns**:
- **Total_Cost_Bn**: Cumulative total cost for airlines
- **Green_Premium_vs_S0_Bn**: Extra cost compared to business-as-usual
- **Total_CO2_Avoided_vs_S0_Mt**: Total emissions reductions (same for S1 & S2)
- **Cost_per_tCO2_Abated_USD**: Cost efficiency — lower is better
- **Avg_SAF_Price_USD_per_t**: Average price airlines pay for SAF

**Why S2 is better**:
- ✅ **S2 costs $1,393B vs S1's $1,681B** — saves $287 billion for airlines
- ✅ **S2 cost per tCO2: $29/t vs S1's $633/t** — 95% more cost-efficient
- ✅ **S2 average SAF price: $898/t vs S1's $2,058/t** — 56% cheaper for airlines
- ✅ **Same emissions reductions** as S1 (476.3 Mt CO2 avoided)

### 2. Year-Specific Summary (2030)

**File**: `table_year_summary_2030.png`

**What it shows**: Detailed cost breakdown for a single year (2030), showing exactly where costs come from.

**Key columns**:
- **Total_Cost_Bn**: Total annual cost
- **Fuel_Cost_Bn**: Cost of fuel (Jet A-1 + SAF)
- **Carbon_Cost_Bn**: EU ETS carbon costs
- **Logistics_Cost_Bn**: Transportation penalty (S1 & S2 only)
- **Levy_Revenue_Bn**: Passenger levy revenue (S2 only)
- **SAF_Price_USD_per_t**: What airlines actually pay per tonne of SAF

**Why S2 is better**:
- ✅ **S2 costs $44.67B vs S1's $53.69B** — 17% cheaper
- ✅ **S2 even beats S0 ($48.57B)** — cheaper than business-as-usual thanks to subsidies
- ✅ **S2 SAF price: $0/t** (fully subsidized) vs S1's $2,130/t
- ✅ **Same emissions reductions** as S1 (7.3 Mt CO2 avoided in 2030)

## Visualizations

### 3. Total Cost by Scenario Over Time

**File**: `total_costs_scenario_comparison.png`

**What it shows**: Line chart comparing annual total costs for S0, S1, and S2 from 2026 to 2050.

**Key insights**:
- **S1 (red line)**: Consistently above S0 — mandate is expensive
- **S2 (green line)**: Stays close to S0 and well below S1 — Smart Levy makes decarbonization affordable
- **The gap widens over time** as SAF volumes grow, but S2 subsidies keep costs manageable

**Why S2 is better**: S2 delivers the same emissions reductions as S1 but at a fraction of the cost, staying competitive with business-as-usual.

---

### 4. Cost Breakdown for 2030

**File**: `cost_breakdown_2030.png`

**What it shows**: Stacked bar chart breaking down costs into components (fuel, carbon, logistics, levy) for each scenario in 2030.

**Key components**:
- **Blue**: Fuel costs
- **Orange**: Carbon costs
- **Red**: Logistics penalty (waste in S1 & S2)
- **Translucent**: Levy revenue (passenger burden in S2)

**Why S2 is better**:
- ✅ **Lower fuel costs** thanks to SAF subsidies
- ✅ **Same logistics penalty** as S1 (physical constraint)
- ✅ **Levy is paid by passengers**, not airlines — making S2 cheaper for the aviation industry
- ✅ **Total bar is shorter** than S1, showing overall cost savings

---

### 5. Cost per tCO₂ Abated (S1 vs S2)

**File**: `cost_per_tco2_s1_vs_s2.png`

**What it shows**: Line chart comparing the cost efficiency of emissions reductions (USD per tonne CO2 avoided) for S1 vs S2 over time.

**Key insights**:
- **S1 (red line)**: High cost per tonne — expensive way to reduce emissions
- **S2 (green line)**: Much lower cost per tonne — efficient decarbonization
- **The gap is largest in early years** when subsidies are most impactful

**Why S2 is better**: S2 achieves the **same emissions reductions** as S1 but at **95% lower cost per tonne**, making it a much more cost-effective climate solution.

---

### 6. SAF vs Jet A-1 Prices

**File**: `saf_vs_jet_prices.png`

**What it shows**: Line chart comparing three prices over time:
- Jet A-1 (fossil fuel baseline)
- S1 SAF price (blended HEFA + PtL, no subsidies)
- S2 SAF price (post-subsidy, what airlines actually pay)

**Key insights**:
- **Large gap** between Jet A-1 and S1 SAF prices — this is the "green premium"
- **S2 closes the gap** through subsidies, especially in early years
- **S2 price approaches Jet A-1** over time as subsidies scale with levy revenue

**Why S2 is better**: By subsidizing SAF prices, S2 makes sustainable fuel **affordable for airlines**, removing the economic barrier to decarbonization that exists in S1.

---

### 7. HEFA Feedstock Wall & PtL Ramp-Up

**File**: `feedstock_wall_hefa_ptl.png`

**What it shows**: Stacked area chart showing:
- **Blue area**: HEFA (bio-SAF) volumes
- **Orange area**: PtL (e-fuel) volumes
- **Dashed line**: HEFA EU supply cap (5.0 Mt/year) — "the feedstock wall"

**Key insights**:
- **Wall hit in 2031**: When SAF demand exceeds HEFA supply cap
- **PtL ramps up**: Expensive e-fuels must fill the gap after 2031
- **Physical constraint remains**: Even with Smart Levy, HEFA is limited

**Why this matters**: This shows that **S2 doesn't eliminate physical constraints** — it just makes the transition **economically feasible**. The Smart Levy helps airlines afford the expensive PtL that becomes necessary after hitting the feedstock wall.
